# Forum 5 — Instructions

*Week 12 — Other Techniques*

For how this activity works in class and how to submit it, see [Forum Instructions]({{ '/forum/' | relative_url }}).

## Reading

Read **Chapter 5: "How Counting Changes Hearts and Minds"** from *Counting: How We Use Numbers to Decide What Matters* by Deborah Stone.

Stone's argument: measuring something changes the people and things being measured — the "Fitbit Effect." Wearing a step counter changes how you walk. Counting the hours to the South Pole narrowed an explorer's experience of the journey itself. Labeling food by nutrient counts turned eating from a cultural pleasure into a guilt-driven metric. Once you optimize for a number, the number starts substituting for the thing it was supposed to represent.

## Discussion Questions (~25 min)

Discuss and write a response to **each** question below on your worksheet — this isn't a pick-one; plan on roughly 25 minutes total to work through all four.

1. Goodhart's Law says that once a measure becomes a target, it stops being a good measure. Where have you seen a machine learning metric (accuracy, recall, engagement) become the target itself, at the expense of the outcome it was meant to track?
2. Techniques like resampling or adjusting decision thresholds change *how a model sees* an imbalanced dataset. Stone's "Fitbit Effect" is about measurement changing behavior — is there an analogous effect where changing how a model is trained changes what a model "believes" is normal or rare in the world?
3. Time-series models are trained on the past to predict the future. Stone would ask what gets narrowed or distorted once an institution starts optimizing decisions around that prediction. What's an example of a forecast that, once acted on, changed the very pattern it predicted?
4. **Compare notes.** After discussing with a classmate, write down one point where their answer differed from yours, or one idea of theirs you hadn't considered — in their words, noting whose idea it was.
